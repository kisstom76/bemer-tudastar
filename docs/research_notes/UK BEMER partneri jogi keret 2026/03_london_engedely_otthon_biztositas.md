# London special treatment licensing, home business and insurance for PEMF (BEMER) sessions: fact-check of the Feb 2026 Gemini report (status 13 Oct 2026)

Scope: a person without medical qualifications (one of them a beautician) offering paid BEMER/PEMF sessions in London, in a salon, at home or as a mobile service, plus demos at exhibitions. Each claim gets a verdict, the current fact and a source.
Research date: 2026-10-13. Borough PDFs were downloaded and searched in full text (pdftotext) for "magnet", "PEMF", "electromagnetic", "BEMER", "light", "electric" and "frequency".

---

## Claim 1 – LLAA 1991 Special Treatment Licence; PEMF probably counts as "electric treatment"; do borough lists name PEMF or magnetic therapy?

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct.** The licensing duty and the "of a like kind" catch-all are real. Gemini's quoted wording is incomplete, though: the statutory list also covers cosmetic piercing, chiropody, vapour, sauna and other baths. **None of the borough A–Z lists checked names PEMF, magnetic therapy or BEMER**, and no borough was found that either exempts magnetic field therapy or includes it by name. On the London Special Treatment Group's own rule, a treatment missing from the list has simply not been assessed yet. Whether PEMF counts as "electric treatment" is therefore an inference, not a published classification.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory definition (Part II s.4 LLAA 1991), as quoted by the councils: "Massage, manicure, acupuncture, tattooing, cosmetic piercing, chiropody, light, electric, vapour, sauna or other baths or treatments of a like kind." — [Bexley A–Z of Special Treatments, Oct 2024](https://bexley.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-10/a-z-of-special-treatments-october-2024_0.pdf); [Wandsworth A–Z, Jan 2020](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/media/1844/a_z_list_of_treatments.pdf)
- Wandsworth A–Z (Jan 2020, compiled by the London "Special Treatment Group" of most of the 32 boroughs) says the list "is not exhaustive". A treatment that is not on it is not thereby exempt; it "has not been assessed". Individual boroughs "may wish to interpret some treatments differently". — [Wandsworth A–Z](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/media/1844/a_z_list_of_treatments.pdf)
- A full-text search of the Wandsworth (Jan 2020) and Bexley (Oct 2024) lists found **no entry for "magnetic", "PEMF" or "BEMER"**. — same two PDFs
- Closest analogues in Wandsworth: Bioresonance therapy is **not licensable** (data sheet 12, 22.11.2007). The stated reason is that "no artificial energy is introduced into the body". Crystal/Electro-crystal healing is also not licensable. Licensable as "Electric": SCENAR therapy, Microcurrent, High Frequency and Radio Frequency. Licensable as "Light": Infra-red, Colour therapy, Ultra violet, Lasers/IPL. Lumi lift/Lumi facials are licensable as Electric/Light. — [Wandsworth A–Z](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/media/1844/a_z_list_of_treatments.pdf)
- Bexley (Oct 2024): Bioresonance and Crystal/Electro-crystal healing are marked "This does not require a licence". Microcurrent, SCENAR, electrolysis and electrocautery are Category B. LED light therapy (Lumi Facial) is Category C, licensable. Infrared is Category C. Lasers/IPL are Category A. Bexley also states that a treatment missing from the list "does not automatically mean" it can be offered without a licence, and asks for such enquiries to go to Trading Standards. — [Bexley A–Z, Oct 2024](https://bexley.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-10/a-z-of-special-treatments-october-2024_0.pdf)
- Westminster's licensing page links its own A–Z of treatments (a 64-page PDF) and repeats that missing treatments may simply not have been assessed yet. — [Westminster special treatment premises licences](https://westminster.gov.uk/licensing/other-licence-and-business-permit-types/special-treatment-premises-licences)
- Tower Hamlets describes special treatments as "including, but not limited to" massage, manicure, acupuncture, tattooing, cosmetic piercing, chiropody, light and electric treatments, plus vapour, sauna and infra-red cabin baths. — [Tower Hamlets](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/lgnl/business/licences/special_treatments_licence.aspx)
- The City of London uses a different statute (London County Council (General Powers) Act 1920). Its list is not exhaustive either, and it asks for enquiries about unlisted treatments. — [City of London guidance notes](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Business/licensing-mst-treatment-guidance-notes.pdf)
- Without a licence, providing or advertising special treatments is an offence. Barking & Dagenham cites a fine of up to £5,000. — [LBBD](https://www.lbbd.gov.uk/special-treatment-licences)

### Inferences
- PEMF does introduce energy (a pulsed magnetic field generated from mains power) into the body. That separates it from bioresonance, which the lists treat as unlicensable precisely because "no artificial energy is introduced". Electrical body-applied devices such as SCENAR and microcurrent are classed as licensable "Electric". A borough asked about BEMER is therefore more likely to treat it as licensable "electric … of a like kind" than to exempt it. This is a reasoned guess. No borough has ruled on it.
- In practice: before starting, ask the licensing team of the borough concerned **in writing** for a classification, naming the device as a "BEMER PEMF device, mains-powered, applied via body mat/applicator". Keep the reply on file.

### Gaps
- The Hammersmith & Fulham A–Z (June 2023 PDF at the cited URL) returned 404, so its current version was not checked. Westminster's 64-page A–Z, Camden's and Tower Hamlets' full lists were not text-searched. They are unlikely to differ, because all of them derive from the London Special Treatment Group list, but this is unverified.
- No published borough decision on PEMF, magnetic therapy or BEMER was found.

## Claim 2 – BEMER B.Light falls under "light treatment"

### Takeaway
**Verdict: probably correct, but unverifiable for the device itself.** Light-based treatments, LED included, are explicitly licensable in the borough lists. B.Light is not named anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Bexley: "Lumi Facial/LED Light Therapy – Category C" is licensable, and "Where LED light therapy is involved, this will require a licence under Category C". — [Bexley A–Z, Oct 2024](https://bexley.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-10/a-z-of-special-treatments-october-2024_0.pdf)
- Wandsworth: Infra-red, Colour therapy and UV are licensable as "Light". Lumi facials are licensable as Electric/Light. — [Wandsworth A–Z](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/media/1844/a_z_list_of_treatments.pdf)

### Inferences
- If the B.Light module is used on clients in a London premises, it is very likely a separately listed "light" treatment and should be named in the licence or variation application.

### Gaps
- No council statement on B.Light specifically.

## Claim 3 – The licence is treatment-specific; adding a treatment needs a variation

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct.** Fee schedules include separate "Variation" and "Removal of treatment" application types, and Waltham Forest charges per extra treatment.

### Cited Findings
- Westminster (fees effective 1 April 2026): Variation £840, Removal of treatment £224, Transfer £336, Change of details £196. — [Westminster Licensing Fees 2026, v1.1 04/2026, p.23](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/media/document/licensing-fees-2026)
- Waltham Forest (from 1 Feb 2026): minimum £590 for premises with up to 2 treatments (excluding laser/IPL), plus £152 per extra treatment, capped at £840 for that category. — [Waltham Forest](https://www.walthamforest.gov.uk/businesses/licences-and-street-trading/licence-provide-massage-and-special-treatment) (via search snippet; page not fetched in full)
- Tower Hamlets: therapists working under the licence are named on it, and new therapists are added by application. — [Tower Hamlets](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/lgnl/business/licences/special_treatments_licence.aspx)

### Gaps
- None material.

## Claim 4 – Accepted qualifications; no NVQ for BEMER; manufacturer certificate at the council's discretion

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct in substance.** The lists' qualification column is "for guidance only". Manufacturer training is explicitly accepted for some devices (e.g. lasers/IPL, as a supplement to a core qualification). No borough list names a BEMER or PEMF qualification. Whether a manufacturer's certificate is accepted is therefore a case-by-case decision by the council.

### Cited Findings
- "Qualifications – these are for guidance only." — [Wandsworth A–Z](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/media/1844/a_z_list_of_treatments.pdf)
- For Lasers/IPL, Wandsworth requires a "core of knowledge and manufacturer training". This is precedent that councils accept manufacturer training as part of the evidence. — same source
- Tower Hamlets requires copies of current qualification certificates for each therapist. — [Tower Hamlets](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/lgnl/business/licences/special_treatments_licence.aspx)
- Barnet's licensing policy for 2022–2027 bands fees by risk level and sets qualification standards. — [Barnet Special Treatment Licensing Policy 2022–2027](https://barnet.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s68582/Special%20Treatment%20Licensing%20Policy%20-%20Appendix.pdf)

### Inferences
- A beautician's existing Level 2/3 beauty qualification, especially one covering electrical facial/body treatments, plus BEMER manufacturer training is the strongest package to present. This is not a guarantee.

### Gaps
- Whether an Ofqual-regulated PEMF qualification exists in 2026 was not verified. The "no NVQ" claim is plausible, but no source confirms it.

## Claim 5 – Home premises must meet licence conditions; fee about £300–£900 per year

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct / outdated.** Home premises do need a licence if clients come there; Hounslow explicitly says "from your home". The £300–£900 range is too low for central boroughs: **Westminster charges £2,912 for a new lower-risk licence** from April 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Westminster** (effective 1 April 2026), lower-risk premises: new £2,912 (Part A £1,344 + Part B £1,568), renewal £2,240 (£896 + £1,344), variation £840. Higher-risk premises: new £3,136, renewal £2,240. — [Westminster Licensing Fees 2026](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/media/document/licensing-fees-2026)
- **Tower Hamlets** (current page): new or renewal £732; laser/IPL £941; adding therapists £150 per application since 1 April 2026. — [Tower Hamlets](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/lgnl/business/licences/special_treatments_licence.aspx)
- **Waltham Forest** (from 1 Feb 2026): £590 minimum for up to 2 treatments, +£152 per extra treatment, up to £840; additional operators £101 each. — [Waltham Forest](https://www.walthamforest.gov.uk/businesses/licences-and-street-trading/licence-provide-massage-and-special-treatment) (search snippet)
- **Redbridge** 2025/26 schedule: laser/IPL renewal £623; new licences pro-rata. — [Redbridge fees 25-26](https://www.redbridge.gov.uk/media/impehjad/special-treatment-fees-25-26.pdf) (search snippet)
- **Hounslow**: the licence applies to treatments "from premises or from your home". — [Hounslow](https://hounslow.gov.uk/info/20071/licensing/1214/massage_and_special_treatment_licences)

### Inferences
- A realistic range for 2026/27 is about £600–£950 in outer and east London boroughs and about £2,200–£3,100 in Westminster. Westminster's application fee is split, with Part A non-refundable.

### Gaps
- Camden, Hammersmith & Fulham and Kensington & Chelsea 2026/27 fees were not retrieved.

## Claim 6 – Mobile therapists: some boroughs require registration, others don't

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct.** A mobile-only service (treating clients only in their own homes) generally does not need a premises licence. Bromley and Lewisham require a (free) therapist registration; Kensington & Chelsea has a therapist registration form.

### Cited Findings
- Lewisham: mobile therapists are not required to hold a licence. Therapists working in Lewisham must register with the council (photo, qualification certificates, public liability insurance if held), at no fee. — [Lewisham](https://lewisham.gov.uk/myservices/business/licences-and-street-trading/licences-issued-by-the-licensing-team/special-treatments-licences); [Lewisham mobile therapist guidance](https://lewisham.gov.uk/-/media/services/business/licensing/special-treatments-licence/mobiletherapistguidance.pdf)
- Bromley: a mobile beautician visiting clients' homes needs no licence, but "every therapist that works within the borough will need to be registered". — [Bromley](https://bromley.gov.uk/licences/special-treatments-licence)
- Hounslow: no licence is needed if treatments are carried out only in the client's own home and given only to that client. — [Hounslow](https://hounslow.gov.uk/info/20071/licensing/1214/massage_and_special_treatment_licences)
- CNHC: no Special Treatment licence is needed for a mobile-only service (vehicle or client's home), but "you may need to register with your local council". — [CNHC](https://www.cnhc.org.uk/special-treatment-licences-london)
- RBKC publishes a "Therapist Registration Form". — [RBKC form](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/wamdocs/Special%20Treatment%20-%20Therapist%20Registration%20Form%20%28New%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- A mobile PEMF service is the lowest-friction route in London, provided the therapist checks each borough she works in for a registration duty.

### Gaps
- No full table of all 32 boroughs' mobile-registration rules exists.

## Claim 7 – Exemptions for doctors and certain bodies (CNHC etc.)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct, and irrelevant for BEMER.** The exemptions exist, but they are discipline-specific. CNHC's exemption covers only aromatherapy, Bowen, massage (including sports massage), microsystems acupuncture, reflexology and shiatsu. **No exempt body covers PEMF or magnetic therapy.** A CNHC-registered massage therapist offering BEMER would still need a licence for the BEMER part.

### Cited Findings
- CNHC exemption: only for the disciplines listed above. The exemption is advisory and councils may still require a licence or an exemption application. Other exemptions: treatments by or under the supervision of a GMC-registered doctor; acupuncture by a dentist; hospitals and nursing homes; premises giving treatment free of charge. — [CNHC special treatment licences](https://www.cnhc.org.uk/special-treatment-licences-london) (page footer © 2008–2026)
- Tower Hamlets: members of exempt organisations are exempt "only for the treatments listed against that organisation". GMC-doctor-supervised premises "may" be exempt. — [Tower Hamlets](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/lgnl/business/licences/special_treatments_licence.aspx); [Tower Hamlets exempt bodies list](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/Documents/Consumer-affairs/Registration,-certification-and-licensing/Licensing/List-of-bodies-of-health-practitioners-exemption-list.docx)
- HCPC registrants, chiropractors and osteopaths are exempt by statute (s.4 LLAA 1991). — summarised in [Havering exemptions list](https://www.havering.gov.uk/downloads/file/4047/special_treatment_licence_exemptions); [Richmond exempted bodies](https://www.richmond.gov.uk/media/21013/special_treatments_exempted_bodies.pdf)

### Gaps
- The borough exemption lists found date from 2017–2021 (CNHC document April 2018). No 2026 consolidated London Special Treatment Group list was found.

## Claim 8 – ExCeL / Olympia hold the permissions, so exhibitors need no licence for demos

### Takeaway
**Verdict: wrong or unsupported as stated.** Newham (ExCeL) runs a **separate "Special Treatment Licence (Shows/Events)" application**, filed per stand. It covers a maximum of 12 months and requires a 28-day consultation. Show organisers (e.g. AM London) make **practitioner registration compulsory** for exhibitors who perform treatments. No source was found saying that the venue's permissions cover exhibitors.

### Cited Findings
- Newham "Application for Special Treatment Licence (Shows/Events)" under LLAA 1991 Part 2 asks for the show name, date and stand number. A new licence lasts up to 12 months; there is a 28-day consultation and a separate renewal form. — [Newham shows/events form](https://www.newham.gov.uk/downloads/file/6512/mst-show-and-events-application)
- AM London exhibitor manual: "Practitioner Registration" is "Compulsory for exhibitors who will be conducting treatments". It points to Newham's A–Z of licensable treatments. — [AM London exhibitor manual](https://aestheticmed.co.uk/am-london-exhibitor-manual)
- Tower Hamlets charges a separate tattoo-convention licence (£3,135). This shows that events are licensed in their own right. — [Tower Hamlets](https://www.towerhamlets.gov.uk/lgnl/business/licences/special_treatments_licence.aspx)

### Inferences
- If the device is only **shown** (no client treated, no charge, e.g. the exhibitor lying on the mat herself), there is arguably no "special treatment" being provided. Free treatments also fall under the CNHC-noted "no gain or reward" premises exemption, but that exemption must be confirmed with the borough. For hands-on demo sessions on the public at ExCeL, plan for Newham's shows/events licence plus the organiser's practitioner registration.
- Olympia is in Kensington & Chelsea (or on the Hammersmith & Fulham border). Ask that council and the organiser.

### Gaps
- No RBKC/Olympia-specific event licence document was found. Newham's event fee was not found.

## Claim 9 – Outside London: LG(MP)A 1982 registration; new non-surgical cosmetic licensing scheme

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct for 1982; status of the cosmetic scheme needs an update.** The 1982 Act's registration covers acupuncture, tattooing, semi-permanent skin-colouring, cosmetic piercing and electrolysis, not PEMF. The Health and Care Act 2022 s.180 scheme is **still not in force (Oct 2026)**. The government response was published on 7 Aug 2025 (three tiers: green/amber/red). The indicative green list names LED and IPL as **light-based cosmetic procedures**. PEMF and magnetic therapy are not named, and BEMER is not marketed as a cosmetic procedure.

### Cited Findings
- 2023 consultation, indicative green tier: "microneedling, mesotherapy, intense pulsed light (IPL) and light emitting diode (LED) therapies", described as "not a final or complete list". — [GOV.UK consultation document](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/licensing-of-non-surgical-cosmetic-procedures/the-licensing-of-non-surgical-cosmetic-procedures-in-england)
- Government response on 7 Aug 2025 confirmed the three-tier model. Local authorities will license green and amber; red will be CQC-regulated. Further consultation and secondary legislation are needed. — [CMS Law-Now, Aug 2025](https://cms-lawnow.com/en/ealerts/2025/08/regulating-beauty-what-the-government-s-consultation-means-for-non-surgical-procedures); [House of Commons Library CBP-10331](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10331/)
- Written answer HL12386 (27 Nov 2025): the government intends to consult on restricting the highest-risk procedures in spring 2026. — [UK Parliament written answer HL12386](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2025-11-27/HL12386/)
- One industry blog (HealSuite, 1 July 2026) claims that parts of the regime have been live since July 2026. Other sources contradict this, and it was not confirmed on GOV.UK. Treat it as unreliable. — [HealSuite blog](https://healsuite.ai/blog/englands-aesthetics-licensing-scheme-starts-in-july-2026)
- LG(MP)A 1982 Part VIII (ss.13–17): registration for acupuncture, tattooing, semi-permanent skin-colouring, cosmetic piercing and electrolysis. — [legislation.gov.uk LG(MP)A 1982](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1982/30/part/VIII) (from prior knowledge; not re-fetched this session)

### Inferences
- Outside London, a PEMF wellness session currently needs no specific local-authority treatment licence. If B.Light is ever marketed as a cosmetic (skin) treatment, it could fall within the future green-tier "LED" category once the scheme commences.

### Gaps
- Whether the spring-2026 red-tier consultation was actually published could not be confirmed. There is no commencement date.

## Claim 10 – Home business: planning, business rates, SBRR £12,000, mortgage and tenancy

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct; updated figures for 2026.** SBRR still gives 100% relief at RV ≤ £12,000, tapering to £15,000. From 1 April 2026, England moved to **five multipliers**, and a new "Supporting Small Business Relief" caps the loss of SBRR from the 2026 revaluation.

### Cited Findings
- SBRR: no rates if RV ≤ £12,000 and the business uses only one property; relief tapers from 100% to 0% between £12,001 and £15,000. — [GOV.UK small business rate relief](https://www.gov.uk/business-rates-relief/small-business-rate-relief)
- 2026/27 multipliers (from 1 Apr 2026): small-business retail, hospitality and leisure (RHL, RV < £51k) 38.2p; small-business non-RHL 43.2p; standard RHL 43.0p; standard non-RHL 48.0p; RV ≥ £500k 50.8p. — [Babergh 2026/27](https://www.babergh.gov.uk/w/business-rates-2026-27); [Knowsley](https://www.knowsley.gov.uk/business-licences-and-consumers/business-rates/changes-business-rates-1-april-2026)
- Supporting Small Business Relief 2026: the bill increase is capped at the higher of £800 per year or the transitional cap (5% / 15% / 30% for 2026/27). — [Burnley SSBR 2026/27](https://burnley.gov.uk/?p=13939)
- Home business permissions: mortgage provider or landlord; planning authority (if alterations, or if the business changes the character of the home); council (many customers, signage, licences). — [GOV.UK run a business from home](https://www.gov.uk/run-business-from-home)
- Business rates typically do not apply for minor use of part of the home, but can apply if part of the home is converted exclusively for business use. — [Crunch, citing government guidance](https://www.crunch.co.uk/knowledge/article/business-from-rental-property)

### Inferences
- A dedicated treatment room used only for business could be separately rated. Its RV would almost certainly fall under £12,000, giving 100% SBRR, but it must be claimed via the council.

### Gaps
- Whether a beauty or wellness treatment room counts as "RHL" (and so takes the 38.2p multiplier) was not checked.

## Claim 11 – Insurance: public, product and treatment-risk liability; Balens, Salon Gold / Holistic Gold "magnetic therapy" cover

### Takeaway
**Verdict: mostly correct.** Holistic Gold (Salon Gold group) has a dedicated magnetic therapy page that **explicitly names "pulsed electromagnetic field devices"**, from £47.04/year (as shown on the page). Holistic Insurance Services lists "Magnetic Therapy" and "Light Therapy", but not PEMF by name. **Balens' PEMF/magnetic cover could not be verified** (its therapy-list page returned 404).

### Cited Findings
- Holistic Gold magnetic therapy: covers practitioners using "pulsed electromagnetic field devices". £3m public, products and malpractice liability (upgradable to £5m public liability) and £10k financial loss, from £47.04/year including IPT, excluding the admin fee. Optional equipment cover for "PEMF devices, static magnets, magnetic mats". Typically requires evidence of a professional qualification in magnetic therapy or biomagnetism; borderline cases are assessed individually. A freelance/mobile policy is available, plus a "Business at Home" extension. — [Holistic Gold – Magnetic Therapy](https://www.salongold.co.uk/holistic-complementary-therapist-insurance/magnetic-therapy)
- Holistic Insurance Services lists "Magnetic Therapy" and "Light Therapy". It requires a recognised qualification and generally does not accept correspondence or one-day courses; unlisted therapies are assessed on request. — [Holistic Insurance Services therapies](https://www.holisticinsurance.co.uk/therapies/)

### Inferences
- The key underwriting risk is the qualification. BEMER's manufacturer training may count as a "one-day course". Get written confirmation from the insurer that BEMER training is accepted before trading.

### Gaps
- Balens' current treatment list and pricing were not verified.
