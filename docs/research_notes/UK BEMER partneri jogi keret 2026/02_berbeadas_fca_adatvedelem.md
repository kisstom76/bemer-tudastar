# UK: renting out a BEMER device to consumers, brokering hire/finance, and data-protection registration (fact-check of the Feb 2026 Gemini report, status 13 Oct 2026)

Verdict scale: correct / partly correct / wrong / outdated / unverifiable. Sources accessed 13 Oct 2026 unless stated otherwise.

## Claim 1 – Consumer hire is an FCA-regulated activity (hirer an individual, not hire-purchase, agreement CAPABLE of lasting more than 3 months, even if actually used for only 2 weeks; indefinite or auto-renewing contracts caught)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct.** RAO art. 60N defines it exactly this way. The test is whether the agreement is *capable* of lasting more than 3 months, not how long the hire actually runs. Open-ended agreements are caught. Treating renewable agreements as caught is supported by commentary, not by a statute quote. The HM Treasury policy statement of May 2026 confirms that the consumer-hire perimeter is not changing.

### Cited Findings
- RAO art. 60N(1): "Entering into a regulated consumer hire agreement as owner is a specified kind of activity". Under 60N(2), exercising the owner's rights and duties is also a regulated activity. — [legislation.gov.uk, SI 2001/544 art. 60N](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/60N)
- Definition in 60N(3). A "consumer hire agreement" is an agreement between the owner and "an individual or relevant recipient of credit" for the bailment of goods which "is not a hire-purchase agreement" and "is capable of subsisting for more than three months". A "regulated consumer hire agreement" (for agreements from 1 Apr 2014) is any consumer hire agreement that is not exempt under arts. 60O–60Q. — [legislation.gov.uk art. 60N](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/60N)
- A "relevant recipient of credit" / "individual" covers "a partnership consisting of two or three persons not all of whom are bodies corporate, or an unincorporated body of persons which does not consist entirely of bodies corporate and is not a partnership". Sole traders are covered as individuals. — [HM Treasury, Policy statement on reform of the CCA 1974, May 2026, paras 4.86–4.88](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)
- Capability, not actual duration: "The actual duration of the hire for example a 1 week hire is not relevant". An open-ended agreement with no stated duration is treated as capable of lasting over 3 months. A hire "limited to 3 weeks or 3 months" is not regulated by this route. — [The Barrister Group, A. Ali, 14 Jul 2021](https://thebarristergroup.co.uk/blog/how-does-a-hire-agreement-become-regulated)
- Academic commentary on periodic or renewable hire: periodic hire (week-to-week, month-to-month until notice) and a fixed term of 3 months or less with an owner's option to renew beyond 3 months both meet the test. Only a fixed term of 3 months or less with no renewal option falls outside. — [D. Yates, Cambridge Law Journal 2009 (via search summary)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-law-journal/article/application-of-the-consumer-credit-act-1974-to-consumer-hire-agreements/79E7BB9E2A0A50262287645FFF12C4C4)
- "The Government is not making any changes to the regulatory perimeter for consumer hire as part of this consultation." — [HMT policy statement, May 2026, para 4.85](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)
- Note: legislation.gov.uk flags the RAO as "revoked by 2023 c. 29" (FSMA 2023) as a change *yet to be applied*. The revocation is not yet commenced, so the RAO remains the operative text. — [legislation.gov.uk art. 60N](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/60N)

### Inferences
- A sole-trader BEMER partner renting their own device to a private person for an open-ended period or a rolling monthly period needs FCA authorisation, unless an exemption applies.
- A strictly fixed 8-week hire with no right for either party to extend falls outside art. 60N, because it is not capable of lasting more than 3 months.
- The 8-week trials currently advertised in the UK (see Claim 5) are likely designed with this limit in mind. Any "extend if you like" clause would bring them back inside regulation.

### Gaps
- I found no FCA PERG text that explicitly addresses auto-renewal clauses. The "renewal = caught" position rests on commentary (Yates 2009) and on the "capable of" wording, not on an FCA statement.

## Claim 2 – Operating without authorisation is a criminal offence, and the agreement is unenforceable against the hirer (s.26/27 FSMA, court discretion s.28, can the owner recover the goods?)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct, and the mechanism is misstated.**
- The criminal offence is correct: FSMA s.19/s.23, up to 2 years' imprisonment and/or a fine on indictment.
- Unenforceability is correct: FSMA s.26, and s.27 where an unauthorised third party arranged the agreement.
- For credit-related activities, including consumer hire, the discretion to allow enforcement lies with the **FCA under s.28A**, not with the court under s.28. Section 28 expressly excludes credit-related agreements.
- Whether and how the owner gets the goods back was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- s.23 FSMA (breach of the general prohibition): on summary conviction up to 6 months (or the applicable maximum term in a magistrates' court) and/or a fine; on indictment up to 2 years and/or a fine. Defence: the accused "took all reasonable precautions and exercised all due diligence". — [legislation.gov.uk FSMA s.23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/23)
- s.28(1) applies to agreements unenforceable under s.26 or s.27 "other than an agreement entered into in the course of carrying on a credit-related regulated activity". The court-discretion route therefore does **not** apply to consumer hire. Under s.28(7), where s.28 does apply, a party recovering money must "return any other property received by him under the agreement". — [legislation.gov.uk FSMA s.28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/28)
- s.28A: for credit-related agreements that are unenforceable under s.26, 26A or 27, the **FCA**, on the firm's application, may allow the agreement to be enforced or money/property to be retained if "just and equitable". The factors are whether the firm reasonably believed it was not breaching the general prohibition (s.26), or whether the provider knew the third party was breaching it (s.27). Compensation is either agreed by the parties or fixed by FCA notice. — [legislation.gov.uk FSMA s.28A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/28A)
- s.26A, the parallel provision for *authorised* persons acting outside their permission: the agreement is "unenforceable against the other party", who may recover "any money or other property paid or transferred" plus compensation. — [legislation.gov.uk FSMA s.26A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/26A)
- Planned CCA reform: the government will repeal the CCA's *own* automatic sanctions (unenforceability without a court order, disentitlement to interest and default sums) and retain specific CCA criminal offences. These are canvassing off trade premises, circulars to minors, credit reference agencies, pawnbroking, and the debtor/hirer giving information about goods. The FSMA general-prohibition offence and s.26-type unenforceability are a separate regime and are not described as being repealed. — [HMT policy statement, May 2026, para 2.11](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)

### Inferences
- If an unauthorised partner hires out a device under a regulated agreement:
  - the partner cannot enforce payment against the hirer;
  - the hirer can reclaim the rent paid plus compensation (s.26(2), by analogy with the s.26A wording);
  - only the FCA, not a court, can validate the agreement (s.28A).
- s.27 matters for brokering (Claim 6). If an unauthorised partner introduces customers to an authorised BEMER hire scheme, the scheme's agreements can also become unenforceable unless the FCA validates them.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the exact text of s.26 itself, only s.26A, s.28 and s.28A. The s.26(2) recovery wording is assumed to mirror s.26A. Verify against [FSMA s.26](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/26).
- **Can the owner recover the goods?** Not confirmed. s.28(7) (return of property) does not apply to credit-related agreements. Whether the owner can recover the device through a proprietary or tort claim (title stays with the owner), and how s.28A interacts with that, would need a lawyer's opinion. I found no FCA guidance on this point.

## Claim 3 – Exemptions: hire that cannot last more than 3 months; B2B hire to a Ltd company; a low-value hire threshold (£300/year?); chained short contracts

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct.**
- The 3-month point is correct, but it is not an "exemption": such hire is simply outside the definition.
- B2B hire to a company is correct: a body corporate is not an "individual".
- **The "£300 low-value hire exemption" is wrong for consumer hire.** Arts. 60O–60Q contain no such threshold.
- The actual consumer-hire exemptions are: business hire with payments over £25,000 (60O); essential-services/metering (60P); and high-net-worth hirers (60Q).
- Chained contracts: unverifiable. No FCA guidance was found.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 60O (business exemption): exempt if "the hirer is required by the agreement to make payments exceeding £25,000" **and** the agreement is entered into "wholly or predominantly for the purposes of a business". A declaration creates a presumption, which is lost if the owner knows or suspects it is untrue. — [legislation.gov.uk art. 60O](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/60O)
- Art. 60Q (nature of the hirer): exempt if the hirer is an individual who has made an FCA-compliant declaration forgoing CCA protections, supported by a compliant statement of income or assets (the high-net-worth route). There is no £300 threshold. — [legislation.gov.uk art. 60Q](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/60Q)
- PERG refers to an "exemption relating to the supply of essential services" for consumer hire (art. 60P, metering equipment). It is irrelevant to BEMER. — [FCA PERG 2.7.7E / 2.7.19O](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/2/7.html)
- Hire of 3 months or less falls outside the perimeter, and the government is keeping the perimeter unchanged. — [BVRLA analysis (search summary)](https://bvrla.co.uk/home/discover/news-and-analysis/consumer-credit-act-reform-what-the-latest-update-means-for-rental-and-leasing); [HMT para 4.85](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)
- A separate CCA £1,500 figure exists, for the statutory termination right under CCA s.101. There is no statutory termination right within the first 18 months, and none at all where payments exceed £1,500 per year. The government plans to move s.101 into FCA rules. This is a hirer-protection rule, not an exemption. — [HMT policy statement paras 4.32–4.33](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "£300" in the Gemini report may be a confusion with other figures, for example the old £50 small-agreement rules or the borrower-lender-supplier exemptions for credit (art. 60F). It should be deleted from the consumer-hire section.
- Renting to a BEMER customer who is a sole trader or a 2–3-person partnership (e.g. a small therapy practice run as a partnership) is still consumer hire. Only hire to a Ltd/LLP, or business hire over £25,000, escapes.

### Gaps
- **Chained consecutive short contracts:** I found no FCA PERG passage, case or enforcement decision on whether successive hires of 3 months or less are aggregated. Reasoning from the "capable of subsisting" test, a contract that is itself under 3 months but comes with a contractual right or expectation of continuation is at risk. This is inference only, not guidance.

## Claim 4 – FCA "limited permission" for consumer hire: application fee, annual fees (2026/27), business plan, fit and proper

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct in principle; figures need updating.** Consumer hire is a limited-permission activity. For 2026/27:
- application fee: **£560** (pricing category 2);
- CC.1 minimum periodic fee: **£1,100**;
- Financial Ombudsman Service levy for limited consumer credit firms: about **£55**.

A regulatory business plan and financial forecasts are required, and threshold conditions apply (fewer for limited-permission firms).

### Cited Findings
- "Consumer hire is Limited Permission" if the firm only ever enters into consumer hire agreements and exercises owners' rights under them. Limited permission firms are "subject to fewer threshold conditions". Firms already authorised for other activities remain Full Permission. — [FCA, Apply to become a consumer credit lender or hirer – limited permission checklist](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/consumer-credit/prepare/limited-permission-checklist)
- Application fee: limited permission falls in **Category 2 = £560**. Full permission is Category 3 (£1,130), 5 (£5,640) or 6 (£11,260). Fees are non-refundable. Page last updated 13/07/2026. — [FCA, Authorisation and registration application fees](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/apply/fees)
- Category 2 rose from £550 to £560 (a 1% uplift) in the 2026/27 fees instrument FCA 2026/34. — [FCA PS26/14, Appendix (FEES 3 Annex 1A)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-14.pdf)
- Annual minimum fee 2026/27: "CC.1 (limited consumer credit permission) £1,100"; CC.2 (full) £2,200. 2026/27 is "the final year of uplifting our minimum fees", after which they rise in line with FCA costs (2027/28 "increased in line with ORA"). A variable fee applies only above £250,000 regulated income. PS26/14 was published 2 Jul 2026. — [FCA PS26/14, Table 2.2 and para 2.6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-14.pdf)
- FOS general levy, industry block I019 "Consumer credit: limited", flat levy: £55 per firm in the 2026/27 column versus £45 in 2025/26. The table header labels it "Proposed 2026/27", so check the final FOS figure on the invoice. — [FCA PS26/14, FOS levy table](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-14.pdf)
- Required documents: application form, regulatory business plan (market position and comparison with competitors), financial forecasts, and other supporting forms. — [FCA limited permission checklist](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/consumer-credit/prepare/limited-permission-checklist)

### Inferences
- First-year cost of direct authorisation is roughly £560 (application) + £1,100 (minimum fee) + about £55 (FOS levy) ≈ £1,700. This excludes small CC.1 levies (e.g. the illegal money lending levy) and compliance or advisory costs.
- The annual minimum alone (£1,100) is a significant overhead against a single BEMER device.

### Gaps
- Statutory determination period: FSMA s.55V (6 months for a complete application, 12 months for an incomplete one) was not re-verified in this session.
- I did not verify which 2025/26 CC.1 minimum fee the Gemini report quoted. The 2025/26 figure was not extracted from PS26/14.

## Claim 6 – Brokering a central BEMER hire/finance scheme for commission may require credit broking permission (secondary credit broking / limited permission)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct in substance.** Introducing an individual to a person who offers consumer hire is credit broking under FCA PERG. If the scheme's agreements are regulated and the introducer is unauthorised, s.27 FSMA unenforceability risk falls on the scheme operator.

### Cited Findings
- PERG 2.7.7E(2): introducing an individual who wants a consumer hire agreement to another person is credit broking, except where the essential-services exemption would apply. — [FCA PERG 2.7](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/2/7.html)
- s.28A FSMA covers agreements unenforceable under s.27 (made by an authorised provider through an unauthorised third party). The FCA's validation factor is whether the provider knew the third party was breaching the general prohibition. — [legislation.gov.uk FSMA s.28A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/28A)
- Application fee for limited permission (which covers secondary credit broking) is Category 2 (£560). Brokers with full permission pay Category 3, 5 or 6. — [FCA application fees](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/apply/fees)

### Inferences
- If BEMER (or a UK entity) ran an FCA-authorised central hire or finance scheme, a partner earning commission for introductions would be credit broking. The partner would need its own limited permission (secondary credit broking, where the main business is selling goods) or to become an **appointed representative** of the authorised scheme operator.
- If the scheme is for hire of 3 months or less (unregulated), introductions to it are not regulated credit broking.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the RAO credit-broking article (art. 36A) or the exact limited-permission conditions for "secondary credit broking" in this session. Verify on the [FCA consumer credit brokers page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/consumer-credit-brokers).
- Whether the appointed-representative route is available for this model was not verified.

## Claim 7 – CCA reform (HM Treasury phase 1/2 consultations 2025–2026): any change affecting consumer hire as of October 2026

### Takeaway
**Verdict: outdated / needs updating (changed since February 2026).**
- HM Treasury published its **policy statement in May 2026** and **dropped the planned Phase 2 consultation**.
- The consumer-hire definitions are **retained** and the perimeter is **unchanged**.
- s.101 (hirer's termination right) is to move into FCA rules.
- The CCA's automatic sanctions are to be repealed.
- Nothing has yet taken legal effect. Legislation is to come via the Financial Services and Markets Bill announced in the King's Speech on 13 May 2026, with commencement only after the FCA makes replacement rules.

### Cited Findings
- The government had planned a Phase 2 consultation but "considers that it has sufficient evidence to take forward changes in some areas without the need for further consultation". — [HMT policy statement, May 2026, para 2.12](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)
- "Consumer hire agreements" are listed among the provisions retained in legislation. — [HMT para 2.14](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)

- s.101 is to be repealed and recast into FCA rules. Current law gives no statutory termination right in the first 18 months, and none where payments exceed £1,500 per year. — [HMT paras 4.32–4.33](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a06f7505f39105e0848a32a/HM_Treasury_-_Policy_statement_on_reform_of_the_Consumer_Credit_Act_1974.pdf)

### Inferences

## Claim 8 – Data protection: UK GDPR + DPA 2018; ICO data protection fee tier 1 (£40 or £52?); Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changes

### Takeaway
**Verdict: £52 is correct; £40 is outdated.**
- Since 17 Feb 2025 the fee is Tier 1 **£52**, Tier 2 **£78**, Tier 3 **£3,763**, with £5 off for direct debit. No 2026 change was found.
- The DUAA 2025 is now **fully in force**: all outstanding provisions commenced by 19 June 2026.
- Of practical relevance to small businesses: a **new statutory duty to handle data protection complaints**, in force since 19 June 2026.
- The ICO's fee guidance is "under review" because of the DUAA.

### Cited Findings
- ICO: "Most companies will only need to pay £52 or £78 a year". Large organisations pay £3,763, with £5 off for direct debit. The guidance is noted as under review because of the DUAA. Exemption check: ico.org.uk/fee-checker. — [ICO blog: Data protection fee – what you need to do](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/whats-new/blogs/data-protection-fee-what-you-need-to-do/)
- The Feb 2025 increase: Tier 1 rose from £40 to £52 (first increase since 2018); Tier 2 £78; Tier 3 £3,763. — [Lewis Silkin, 6 Feb 2025](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9); [Acuity Law](https://acuitylaw.com/rise-in-ico-fees-data-protection-fees-february/)
- The direct-debit £5 discount was retained across all tiers. — [gov.uk, Data protection fee regime: government response](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/data-protection-fee-regime-proposed-changes/outcome/data-protection-fee-regime-government-response)
- The DUAA complaints requirement commenced on 19 June 2026, the 12-month commencement point, by which time all outstanding DUAA provisions were in force. Businesses must:
  - provide a way to make a data protection complaint;
  - acknowledge it within 30 days;
  - investigate without undue delay;
  - inform the complainant of the outcome. — [ICO, New data protection complaints law now in force (Jun 2026)](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/new-data-protection-complaints-law-now-in-force/); [ICO, One month to go (May 2026)](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/05/one-month-to-go-what-businesses-need-to-know-to-meet-new-data-law/); [ICO, One year on (Jun 2026)](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/one-year-on-marking-the-12-month-commencement-of-the-data-use-and-access-act/)
- A Bar Council document labelled "July 2026" still shows £40. It is outdated and should not be relied on. — [Bar Council PDF](https://www.barcouncilethics.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Data-Protection-Fees-payable-to-the-ICO-July-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- A sole-trader BEMER partner keeping a customer list or CRM with health-related notes about rental clients is a controller processing special-category (health) data. Such a partner very likely has to pay the fee (Tier 1, £52 or £47 by direct debit) and must now have a complaints procedure.
- The narrow fee exemptions (e.g. processing only for own staff admin, accounts or advertising own goods) are unlikely to cover client health notes. This should be checked with the ICO fee-checker.

### Gaps
- The current Tier 1 eligibility thresholds (historically turnover ≤ £632,000 or ≤ 10 staff) were not re-verified for 2026.
- The exact list of fee exemptions was not retrieved: the ICO main fee page returned little content.
- The DUAA renaming of the ICO to an "Information Commission" (a corporate body) and its timing were not verified.
- No source found for any planned 2026/27 fee change.
