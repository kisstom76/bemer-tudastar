## A1. VAT registration threshold £90,000 rolling 12 months (+ deregistration)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct (still current).** The threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover on a rolling 12-month look-back (plus a 30-day forward-look test). It was not changed in the November 2025 Budget (frozen); deregistration threshold £88,000 (secondary source only). No evidence found of a change in April 2026.

### Cited Findings
- GOV.UK (current page, no "last updated" date shown, read 2026-10-13): register if total taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000; register within 30 days of the end of the month you went over; effective date = first day of the second month after. Separate forward test: register if turnover will exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone — [GOV.UK: When to register for VAT](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)
- Taxable turnover = everything sold that is not VAT-exempt or out of scope; includes zero-rated and reduced-rated sales — same page — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)
- £90,000 took effect 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold £88,000 — [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/united-kingdom/uk-raises-vat-registration-threshold-to-90000/) (secondary)
- Threshold left unchanged at the 2025 Autumn Budget (frozen); pre-Budget there was debate about raising to £100k or cutting to ~£30k, neither happened — [ByteStart](https://www.bytestart.co.uk/self-employed-tax/vat-registration-threshold/); [Azets](https://www.azets.com/en-uk/insights/autumn-budget-2025-potential-changes-to-vat) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Because the GOV.UK page still shows £90,000 in Oct 2026, no change took effect in April 2026.

### Gaps
- Whether the Budget 2025 freeze has a stated end date (I have an unverified recollection of a multi-year freeze) — check the Budget 2025 "Overview of Tax Legislation and Rates" (OOTLAR) on gov.uk.
- Deregistration threshold £88,000 not confirmed on a gov.uk page in this session (gov.uk "Cancel VAT registration" page would confirm).

## A2. Reseller (full sale price) vs commission agent (commission only); commission from BEMER Int. AG (Liechtenstein)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct in principle, but incomplete — and the important missing point is favourable.** A partner who buys and resells counts the full sale price; an agent counts only commission. But commission paid by a non-UK business (BEMER Int. AG, Liechtenstein) to a UK sole trader is a B2B service whose place of supply under the general rule is where the customer belongs (Liechtenstein) → outside the scope of UK VAT → does **not** count toward the £90,000 threshold. Which model applies depends on who invoices the end customer (contract question — accountant + contract review).

### Cited Findings
- Taxable turnover excludes VAT-exempt and "out of scope" supplies — [GOV.UK: When to register](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)
- No UK VAT is chargeable where the place of supply is outside the UK; the general B2B rule puts the place of supply where the customer belongs — [GOV.UK: VAT Notice 741A Place of supply of services](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-place-of-supply-of-services-notice-741a)
- HMRC staff on the HMRC Community forum: for the UK threshold you only look at supplies whose place of supply is in the UK; services to overseas businesses "should not form part of your taxable turnover" — [HMRC Community forum thread](https://community.hmrc.gov.uk/customerforums/vat/a4732f8b-4114-ee11-a81c-000d3a8751e3?page=2) (informal HMRC answer, not binding guidance)
- Caution — the threshold DOES include reverse-charge services the UK business *receives* from overseas businesses (purchases, e.g. paying for an overseas software/marketing service) — [GOV.UK: When to register](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)

### Inferences
- If BEMER sells directly to the UK end customer and only pays commission to the partner, a London partner's commission is outside the scope of UK VAT and the partner may never reach the VAT threshold even with high commission income. (The partner can still voluntarily register.)
- If the partner actually buys devices and resells them to UK consumers in their own name, each full retail price (incl. VAT-inclusive pricing considerations) counts; with device prices in the thousands of pounds, £90,000 can be reached with a few dozen sales.
- Mixed model (some resale + commission): only the UK resale turnover counts; the Liechtenstein commission does not.
- Note the Gemini document's framing ("commission counts toward the threshold") is wrong for commission from a non-UK principal; it is right only for commission from a UK principal.
- Liechtenstein side: BEMER Int. AG would account for any VAT under its own rules (Liechtenstein uses the Swiss VAT system) — not a UK partner issue, but unverified.

### Gaps
- Whether BEMER UK sales are invoiced by BEMER Int. AG (Liechtenstein) or a UK entity, and whether partners ever buy for resale — the contract wording ("sales agent" + purchase/resale) must be checked; another researcher covers the contract.
- Whether any Schedule 4A special rule (e.g. intermediary services) could apply — the general rule should cover intermediary services B2B, but an accountant should confirm.

## A3. Sole trader vs Ltd; 2026/27 rates; dividends; NIC; Making Tax Digital

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct / partly outdated.** Income tax 20/40/45% and corporation tax 19–25% with marginal relief are still correct for 2026/27. **Outdated:** dividend tax rates rose by 2 points from 6 April 2026 (8.75%→10.75% basic, 33.75%→35.75% higher; additional 39.35% unchanged), reducing the Ltd advantage. Class 2 NIC is no longer payable compulsorily (since April 2024); Class 4 is 6%/2%. MTD for Income Tax became mandatory from 6 April 2026 for qualifying income over £50,000 (gross, self-employment + property), £30,000 from April 2027, £20,000 from April 2028.

### Cited Findings
- Dividend rates from April 2026: basic 10.75%, higher 35.75%, additional unchanged at 39.35%, dividend allowance unchanged; property and savings income rates +2 points from April 2027 (22/42/47%) — [GOV.UK technical note: Change to tax rates for property, savings and dividend income](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-tax-rates-for-property-savings-and-dividend-income/change-to-tax-rates-for-property-savings-and-dividend-income-technical-note); [LITRG Budget 2025 summary](https://www.litrg.org.uk/news/budget-2025-summary-main-personal-tax-announcements-and-what-they-mean-you)
- Corporation tax 2026/27: 19% small profits rate up to £50,000; marginal relief £50,001–£250,000 (effective marginal rate 26.5% in the band); 25% main rate above £250,000; limits divided by number of associated companies — [Accotax 2026/27 guide](https://accotax.co.uk/?p=106433); [uktax.tools](https://uktax.tools/tax-insights/corporation-tax-rates-2026-27-marginal-relief/) (secondary)
- Class 2 NIC no longer compulsory since April 2024 (treated as paid above the Small Profits Threshold £7,105); voluntary Class 2 £3.65/week in 2026/27; Class 4: 6% on profits £12,570–£50,270, 2% above — [ByteStart](https://www.bytestart.co.uk/self-employed-tax/national-insurance/); [betteraccount](https://betteraccount.co.uk/?p=2345) (secondary; one other site gives older figures — conflicting source: [payslipiq](https://payslipiq.co.uk/guides/self-employed-class-2-and-4-ni) shows 8%/£3.45, which are pre-2024 figures)
- MTD ITSA: from 6 April 2026 for qualifying income over £50,000 (based on 2024-25), £30,000 from April 2027 (based on 2025-26), £20,000 from April 2028; qualifying income = gross self-employment + property income before expenses; quarterly digital updates via compatible software — [GOV.UK press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/one-year-until-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-launches); [HMRC manual SALF1440](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/self-assessment-legal-framework/salf1440)
- MTD for Corporation Tax will not go ahead — [Sage](https://www.sage.com/en-gb/blog/making-tax-digital-thresholds-vat-itsa-corporation-tax/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Income tax 20/40/45% bands (personal allowance £12,570, frozen) remain — not re-verified on gov.uk in this session but consistent with all sources above (Class 4 limits mirror the income tax bands).
- For a typical part-time BEMER partner (profit well under £50k) a sole tradership is simpler; the Ltd route has lost some appeal because of the April 2026 dividend rise. Accountant decision.
- For a Hungarian partner arriving in 2026, MTD applies from the tax year after their qualifying income (on a filed return) exceeds the threshold; gross income ≥£20k from 2026-27 brings them into MTD from April 2028.

### Gaps
- MTD penalty-holiday details (first-year leniency) not confirmed on gov.uk.
- Exact 2026/27 personal allowance / band figures not fetched from gov.uk "Income Tax rates and allowances" page in this session.

## A4. IR35 / off-payroll working

### Takeaway
**Verdict: largely irrelevant (Gemini over-emphasised it).** IR35 applies only where a worker provides personal services through an intermediary (typically their own Ltd) to a client in an employment-like relationship. A sole trader is not within IR35; a partner earning commission from many end-customer sales via BEMER is unlikely to look like a disguised employee. No source specific to network-marketing partners found.

### Cited Findings
- No primary source fetched in this session (gov.uk "Understanding off-payroll working (IR35)" would be the reference).

### Inferences
- If a partner trades as a sole trader: IR35 does not apply by definition (no intermediary); the only question would be general employment status, which commission-only, self-organised selling makes unlikely.
- If a partner uses their own Ltd: IR35 could in theory be considered, but the off-payroll rules for a medium/large client would require BEMER Int. AG (non-UK) to be the "client" — the outcome is low-risk but needs an accountant's view.

### Gaps
- Not verified against HMRC ESM/off-payroll guidance in this session.

## A5. UK–Hungary DTC 2011, permanent establishment, residence

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct but misframed.** The 2011 UK–Hungary convention (in force 28 Dec 2011; UK income tax effect from 6 April 2012) follows the OECD model: business profits of a Hungarian-resident enterprise are taxable in the UK only via a UK permanent establishment. But for partners who actually **live** in London, the PE analysis is mostly beside the point: they will usually be UK tax resident under the Statutory Residence Test and taxable in the UK on their (worldwide) income; the treaty then only matters for tie-breaking if Hungary also claims residence.

### Cited Findings
- Convention signed Budapest 7 Sept 2011, in force 28 Dec 2011; UK effect 6 April 2012 (income tax/CGT), 1 April 2012 (corporation tax) — [GOV.UK: 2011 UK–Hungary DTC in force](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hungary-tax-treaties/2011-uk-hungary-double-taxation-convention-in-force); [HMRC DT9251](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt9251)
- Art. 5 PE = fixed place of business through which the business is wholly or partly carried on (place of management, branch, office, workshop, etc.); preparatory/auxiliary activities (storage, display, delivery of goods) excluded — [Treaty PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f7e40e5274a2e8ab4c82b/dtr-hungary_-_in_force.pdf)

### Inferences
- A London flat regularly used by a Hungary-resident partner to receive clients/demonstrate devices would very likely be a fixed place of business = PE (consistent with the Art. 5 wording) — Gemini's claim is plausible.
- For partners living in London: under the SRT, spending 183+ days in the UK in a tax year makes you automatically UK resident; having your only home in the UK is another automatic test. UK residents are taxed on worldwide income (the remittance basis was abolished from 6 April 2025 and replaced by the 4-year FIG regime for new arrivals) — **from general knowledge, not re-verified in this session; accountant**.
- Hungarian side: a partner who keeps a Hungarian home/family may also be Hungarian resident → treaty tie-breaker (Art. 4: permanent home, centre of vital interests…) — needs case-by-case advice.

### Gaps
- Art. 7 wording not read directly; SRT (gov.uk RDR3) and FIG regime not fetched in this session.

## B7. Zero-rated VAT relief for chronically sick or disabled people (Notice 701/7) – would BEMER qualify?

### Takeaway
**Verdict: Gemini's description of the conditions is correct; any implication that BEMER can be sold VAT-free is very likely wrong.** All three conditions must hold (eligible person, personal/domestic use, eligible goods); goods must be "designed solely" for disabled people; written eligibility declaration; the supplier (and in practice the manufacturer) decides; the customer cannot reclaim VAT from HMRC afterwards. A device marketed for general wellness, sport and "microcirculation" is a general-purpose item and would fail the "designed solely" test. No HMRC guidance or tribunal case on PEMF/magnetic therapy was found.

### Cited Findings
- Three conditions: eligible customer, personal/domestic use, eligible goods (Notice 701/7 s.2.1); "chronically sick or disabled" = long-term, substantial impairment or condition doctors treat as chronic sickness (e.g. diabetes); temporary conditions excluded; not means-tested, no registration needed (3.2.1) — [GOV.UK: VAT Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017) (last updated 31 Dec 2020)
- "Designed solely": items that merely help a disabled person, or are essential to them, do not qualify; only the designer/manufacturer can confirm, retailers should ask them (4.5); general-purpose items like orthopaedic beds and reclining chairs don't qualify (4.5.1) — [Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017)
- Declaration: written, separate from the order/invoice (HMRC template), can be electronic; don't zero-rate if you believe it inaccurate (3.6–3.9); supplier must take reasonable steps; HMRC gives no binding rulings to retailers (2.3, 3.1) — [Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017)
- Customers cannot claim VAT back from HMRC; only the supplier can correct and refund (2.2) — [Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017)
- HMRC manual: a product designed for everyone with a condition (e.g. back complaints) fails if many with that condition aren't disabled; general-purpose items marketed to disabled people don't qualify; objective test — [HMRC VRDP03400](https://gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-relief-for-disabled-people/vrdp03400); [VRDP38000](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-relief-for-disabled-people/vrdp38000)
- Closest analogue – TENS machines: can be zero-rated when bought by a disabled person for domestic use; sale for short-term pain (childbirth, sports injuries) is standard-rated; Neen Design tribunal: test is whether the device was "designed solely for use by chronic pain sufferers" — [HMRC VRDP31000 Pain relief equipment](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-relief-for-disabled-people/vrdp31000) (this page does not mention magnetic/electromagnetic therapy)
- Recent cases on item 2(g): Nimbus Disability Consultancy [2026] UKFTT 38 (TC) (access card, appeal allowed); Mark Glenn Ltd [2024] UKFTT 715 (hair replacement system, not zero-rated) — [Nimbus](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukftt/tc/2026/38); [Mark Glenn](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukftt/tc/2024/715)

### Inferences
- BEMER is marketed for broad wellness, sport/recovery and sleep (and has a horse line) — this is the opposite of "designed solely for disabled people". Even if the buyer is chronically ill, the goods condition fails. Zero rating is very unlikely to be defensible; a partner offering "VAT-free BEMER" would expose the supplier to an HMRC assessment.
- Only BEMER (manufacturer/supplier) could make the call, with design evidence (Notice 701/7 4.5); a sales partner cannot apply zero rating on their own.
- Since Feb 2026 the Notice itself is unchanged (last updated 2020); the only new material is the Nimbus 2026 tribunal decision, which does not concern medical devices.

### Gaps
- No tribunal decision or HMRC statement on PEMF / magnetic therapy found (searched; none surfaced).
- Whether BEMER has ever sought a VAT position for the UK — unknown.

## B8. Over-60s: 5% reduced rate only for installed mobility aids

### Takeaway
**Verdict: correct.** The reduced rate (5%) applies only where a qualifying mobility aid is supplied **and installed** in the home of a person aged 60+: grab rails, ramps, stair lifts, bath lifts, built-in shower seats/showers containing them, walk-in baths with sealable doors. Age alone gives no VAT relief on a BEMER.

### Cited Findings
- Reduced rate for customers 60+ for qualifying mobility aids supplied and installed in their own/shared home (not residential homes): grab rails, ramps, stair lifts, bath lifts, built-in shower seats, walk-in baths; supplier must supply and install; declaration needed; repairs and general adaptations excluded (Notice 701/7 s.9) — [GOV.UK: Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017)

### Inferences
- The 5% figure is the UK reduced rate; the fetched page summary did not show "5%" explicitly, but the reduced rate in UK VAT is 5%.

### Gaps
- None material.

## B10. Funding: PIP, Access to Work, charity grants

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partly correct, partly outdated.** PIP is not means-tested and can be received while working; it is generally treated as free to spend (no gov.uk restriction found), so a recipient *could* use it toward a device — but **the 2025 "4-point rule" was dropped from the Universal Credit Bill**, and the Timms Review (interim report 9 July 2026; final due autumn 2026) is floating cost-based awards linked to equipment etc. — outcome pending. Access to Work funds work-related specialist equipment, not general wellness devices; unlikely to fund BEMER. Charity grants: MS Society local groups do offer member grants for equipment; no current individual grant schemes found for Arthritis Action or Fibromyalgia Action UK.

### Cited Findings
- PIP: can be received while working, with savings, alongside most other benefits; help with extra living costs — [GOV.UK: PIP](https://www.gov.uk/pip)
- The government withdrew the PIP four-point rule (clause 5) from the bill on 1 July 2025; bill renamed the Universal Credit Bill; PIP descriptor changes deferred to the Timms Review — [Parkinson's UK](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/node/1021296); [Contact](https://contact.org.uk/about-contact/news-and-views/government-announces-changes-to-welfare-reform-bill/)
- UC health element cut for new claimants from April 2026, with protection for existing claimants and severe/terminal conditions (July 2025 source) — [Contact](https://contact.org.uk/about-contact/news-and-views/government-announces-changes-to-welfare-reform-bill/)
- Timms Review interim report (9 July 2026): PIP "no longer fit for purpose"; >38,000 call-for-evidence responses — [GOV.UK news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/first-comprehensive-review-into-pip-finds-it-is-not-fit-for-purpose); September 2026 co-chair update: emerging recommendations include reforms to the current system plus principles for more fundamental reform, within OBR spending projections — [Timms Review co-chair update Sept 2026 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa136a35f6e942efe37ef73/Timms_Review_co_chair_update_September_2026.pdf)
- Reported proposal to base awards on additional costs (equipment, bedding, transport) — [Benefits and Work](https://benefitsandwork.co.uk/news/timms-revives-tory-plan-for-pip-awards-based-on-costs-not-difficulties?rCH=2) (commentary); final recommendations expected autumn 2026 — [Turn2us](https://www.turn2us.org.uk/about-us/news-and-media/latest-news/the-timms-review)
- Access to Work pays for specialist aids/equipment, travel to work and support workers; cannot pay for standard business-setup equipment; reform proposals March 2025, heavy backlogs; NAO report 2026 — [AbilityNet factsheet](https://abilitynet.org.uk/factsheets/how-apply-access-work-grant); [Parliament committee inquiry](https://committees.parliament.uk/work/9516/the-access-to-work-scheme)
- MS Society local groups: grants for health and wellbeing incl. home adaptations, scooters and equipment (local, variable terms) — [MS Society Ayrshire group grants](https://www.mssociety.org.uk/care-and-support/local-support/local-groups/ayrshire-group/grants)
- Fibromyalgia Action UK's charitable purpose is information and support, research; no grant scheme found — [Charity Commission register 1042582](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/about-the-register-of-charities/-/charity-details/1042582)
- Arthritis Action: no individual grant scheme found; recent items are its own fundraising appeals — [Arthritis Action](https://www.arthritisaction.org.uk/?p=40052)

### Inferences
- Telling customers "use your PIP to buy a BEMER" is legally possible today but ethically/regulatory sensitive (selling to vulnerable people; medical claims). If the Timms Review moves to cost-based awards, PIP might become tied to evidenced costs — watch for the final report.
- Access to Work: a BEMER would almost certainly be seen as general-purpose/wellness, not a specialist workplace adjustment → Gemini's suggestion is very likely wrong in practice.
- Charity grants for a premium PEMF device (no NHS/NICE endorsement) are improbable; MS Society local grants are discretionary.

### Gaps
- Royal Assent date and final content of the Universal Credit Act 2025 not confirmed in this session (check legislation.gov.uk).
- Timms Review final report (due autumn 2026) — not yet found as of 13 Oct 2026.
- Current 2026/27 PIP weekly rates not fetched.
- Whether PIP is legally "free to spend" — no gov.uk statement found either way (generally understood as unrestricted; flag as unverified).
